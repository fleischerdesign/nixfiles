# Design

## When to load & job

Use this handbook to turn a brief into a design the human can experience and discuss. The human reviews the design; your job is to make the choices and their consequences visible.

## Inputs

Read the brief first, then the project's design system (`design.md` or `DESIGN.md`, tokens, components, and brand rules), then `references/index.md`. The project takes precedence; never impose a global style.

On existing work, read the running product, not only its documentation. Find the nearest comparable surface, trace its component through variants and tokens, and when documentation and implementation disagree, name the conflict and choose with the human; do not silently make one the standard.

Choose one lead module by deliverable. Add at most two supporting modules, each for a different open design decision. Use the exact paths in the index, resolved relative to this skill's base. Do not read authoring templates at runtime.

If `references/index.md` has no module for the requested deliverable, use the module for the closest medium and name the discipline-specific knowledge it does not cover. Do not invent a module.

## Decisions & process

Before designing, read the lead module and work out which of its decisions matter for this brief. Apply those decisions in the artifact; do not quote the handbook in place of a design.

When an entry links to a method, read that knowledge card before the decision or work step it guides — not after the direction is chosen. Each link gives a reason and an exact `references/knowledge/…` path relative to this skill's base. Load only the cards needed for the work, never the whole set.

Choose the mode from the brief:

- **Open design** — develop two structurally different directions. Choose one and name the trade-off.
- **Given direction** — implement the direction. Show the consequence and what it gives up.
- **Decision brief** — the comparison is the deliverable. Compare options against the baseline and do not force a design.

For an executable design, build a functional slice with real content, then show it in context. For work you cannot execute directly, make a study or briefing artifact and identify who can provide the next proof.

For a small change, start from the existing design, make the targeted change, and show the before-and-after in the context where a regression would appear.

## Typical mistakes & examples

Changing only color leaves the same design decision underneath. For a service directory, task-first navigation helps people who know what they need; topic-first browsing helps people learn what's available but adds a step for a known task.

Do not invent facts, metrics, testimonials, logos, research, or production specs. If specs are missing, state what you can deliver, what you assumed, and what remains blocked. Never claim production approval.

## Deliverables & questions for the human

Lead with the artifact in context, then explain the key decision and what it costs. When a method changed the design, show the concrete difference at the artifact — a state-table excerpt beside the dialog, not a list of loaded cards. Say what you did not see or try; describe the result without self-assessment labels like “professional,” “accessible,” or “print-ready.” Ask only questions whose answers would change the outcome. Iterate on the human's judgment and respond in the brief's language.
