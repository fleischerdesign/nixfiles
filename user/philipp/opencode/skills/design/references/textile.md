# Textile

## When to load & job

Use this for a textile concept where motif, repeat, direction, and hand shape the end use. Produce a tiled pattern study with an end-use overlay and a mill or printer sampling brief. Let fashion lead if the question is how a garment fits rather than how its cloth behaves. A digital field can show pattern relationships, not how the fabric feels or how a supplier's process will reproduce it.

## Inputs & concepts

Collect end-use shapes, typical viewing distances, matching needs at seams or panel joins, handling and care, and physical swatches. Ask the supplier for the base cloth, usable width, process, repeat and color constraints, and how a sample will be made; ask the maker where seams and folds land. With no supplier brief, draw provisional scales and layout rather than assuming width or yield. Describe **hand**, the perceived feel and handling of the cloth, by reference to a named swatch. A draped study cannot on its own describe texture, recovery or response to use.

A **motif** is a design element; a **repeat** is the translation rule for carrying it across the cloth. A straight repeat aligns rows and columns. In a column half-drop, neighboring columns are displaced by half the vertical repeat. If sending the latter as a rectangular straight-repeat tile, the tile must include both column phases, or downstream software will erase the offset. **Directionality** concerns rotation of the design on the cloth; the fabric construction and cut orientation may still constrain rotation even if the motif does not.

## Decisions & procedure

- An obvious repeat gives rhythm when repetition is part of the design. A stagger can soften straight bands, but adjacent panels may need more deliberate matching.
- A directional motif supplies a visual top and bottom. A non-directional version offers greater flexibility where the construction and maker allow rotation, at the cost of that orientation.
- Pair the same pattern with a pliable reference if folds matter and with a firmer reference if a broad surface should hold its definition. A motif that remains legible flat can fragment across tightly spaced folds.

1. Build a **repeat-and-placement sheet** with axis directions, the repeat translation, motif scale, a flat field and end-use overlays for seams, crops and folds. Keep repeat size separate from usable cloth width. If comparing spacing, offset or motif families, use [Parametric variation](knowledge/parametric-variation.md) (`references/knowledge/parametric-variation.md`) so the visible effect can be attributed to the changed parameter.
2. Carry two meaningful alternatives onto the same panel outlines and compare distant and close views. Mark whether the design intends continuity, an offset, or a deliberate break at each join. Convert the consequential join or fold into a sample request.
3. Use [Color management](knowledge/color-management.md) (`references/knowledge/color-management.md`) to identify what a digital review can represent and what color evidence needs the intended cloth. Where choices turn on replacement, care or material impacts, use [Lifecycle scope](knowledge/lifecycle-scope.md) (`references/knowledge/lifecycle-scope.md`) to frame a comparison of equivalent service before making claims.

## Worked example & typical mistakes

A curtain motif has a dark leaf that looks balanced alone but forms stripes across a straight field. Compare a column half-drop: with an illustrative pitch of 20 cm and vertical repeat of 30 cm, shift alternate columns vertically by 15 cm. If the receiving layout uses a rectangular straight tile, show 40 × 30 cm with both phases. Label them A and B on the curtain overlay. A panel cropped to three pitches shows A–B–A; restarting the next at A puts identical phases together. For continuity, start the next at B. Compare that join with an intentional break, then ask for adjacent fabric lengths. A single-tile preview conceals both the initial stripe and the phase reset.

## Standards, limits & handover

Adobe Illustrator Help, “Create and edit patterns,” describes Pattern Options and Brick by Column (`verify` installed version); editor settings are not a mill format. ISO 105-X12:2016 concerns textile color fastness to rubbing, not color matching or overall durability; `verify` applicable edition and scope with the materials specialist if relevant. Supplier values govern usable width, repeat increments and color delivery. The half-drop dimensions are example values, not production defaults.

Show the tile, tiled field, end-use crops and swatch brief. Ask the mill or printer for a sample on the intended cloth; the maker and materials team review joins, color and hand. Final dimensions and production remain open. Ask whether the design must read across the room and whether adjacent panels may interrupt it.
