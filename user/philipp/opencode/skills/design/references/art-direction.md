# Art Direction

## When to load & job

Use this for a visual direction that other people must carry across a set: a portrait series, a launch suite, or a run of editorial covers. Creative direction decides what the work should say; art direction makes that idea recognizable across images made under different conditions. Image making leads when the question is which frame to use or how to capture it; visual design leads when the question is how image, type and content share a surface. Deliver a treatment study in real placements and instructions a photographer, illustrator or retoucher can interpret.

## Inputs & concepts

Get the project’s visual language and message from the creative or brand owner, the placement list and copy zones from the channel owners, and the available assets, usage rights, locations, time and budget from production. Find out which placements are seen together and which must stand alone. Separate references that illustrate a technique from usable artwork; if the library is incomplete, build a study with available material and label unfilled positions rather than implying they are covered.

A **treatment** describes how a picture is made and finished. In portraits that may mean subject scale in frame, camera viewpoint, depth cues, lighting direction and quality, separation from the background, and the relationship between shadows and highlights. Subject scale and viewpoint are different controls: reframing can make a head larger, but it cannot recover a viewpoint that shows more of the sitter's hands or setting. An **invariant** is a relationship that carries recognition across images; a **variable** makes room for a different subject or placement. Name each in terms a maker can draw, light, frame or edit.

## Decisions & procedure

- Constant camera distance and light direction can give different sitters equal emphasis, at the cost of adapting to each person. If individuality matters more, let the framing move and hold the background treatment or tonal relationship instead.
- One close reference can communicate quickly, but invites imitation of incidental wardrobe, set or casting. Several annotated references allow interpretation, provided the maker knows which instruction takes precedence.
- A distinctive hero can set the tone while quieter images keep the sequence from peaking on every page. Where most placements are seen alone, let secondary frames carry more visual weight even if the full suite looks busier.

1. Write a short treatment premise as an observable effect, such as “equal presence for all speakers; surroundings supply the variety.” List what a maker can control on location and what can only be changed in edit.
2. Build a **treatment sheet**: for each cue, give the source reference, what to borrow, what to ignore, how to produce it, and what can move. Put a rough sketch beside cues that are easy to misread, such as camera height or which side receives the main light. For cues shared with the rest of the brand, see [Identity systems](knowledge/identity-systems.md) (`references/knowledge/identity-systems.md`) to separate recognition supplied by the identity from recognition the pictures must carry.
3. Apply the sheet to three unlike subjects in the same intended set. Put the results side by side so differences in subject scale, background and tonal weight are visible. If two cues could carry the set, sketch the same subjects twice: for example, hold head scale in one set and background treatment in the other. For deciding which existing frames can fill the hero and secondary slots, use [Image selection](knowledge/image-selection.md) (`references/knowledge/image-selection.md`).
4. Carry the chosen treatment into representative placements and hand the maker a short brief: capture priorities, edit intent, allowed exceptions and slots that still need commissioning. Distinguish instructions that belong on set from adjustments that can be made after capture.

## Worked example & typical mistakes

A speaker series needs equal presence in a directory, but a craft speaker's hands are important to their story. The roughs show that retaining both hands in the recurring directory crop makes this speaker's face markedly smaller than the others. On the treatment sheet, assign common apparent head scale to directory portraits and allow hands and tools in the wider feature images. For a standalone speaker announcement, use the directory portrait where the name and face are the point; use the feature frame where the placement introduces the craft. If a feature must survive in a compact space, brief a second frame rather than promising to recover the gesture by cropping.

A board of warm portraits without framing notes puts attention on color alone; the resulting directory can alternate tiny faces and tight headshots. Show the grid with shared framing marks and label intentional exceptions.

## Standards, limits & handover

For framing vocabulary, see Michael Freeman, *The Photographer’s Eye*, first edition (2007), chapter 1, “The Image Frame”; this is a method, not a production standard. Adobe Lightroom Classic Help, “[Crop and Straighten photos](https://helpx.adobe.com/lightroom-classic/help/crop-rotate-straighten.html),” documents crop controls; `verify` the installed version before prescribing UI steps. Common head scale here is a treatment convention, not a norm; ratios, delivery formats and reuse permissions come from the project and rights owner.

Show the annotated treatment sheet, selected placements and the set view with fixed and flexible cues marked. A composite cannot establish that location lighting, wardrobe or a complicated frame is achievable. Ask whether series recognition or sitter individuality comes first, and which missing placement justifies a commission. The photographer and producer supply a capture study and confirm what can be made within the brief.
