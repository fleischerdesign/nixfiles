# Art Direction

## When to load & job

Use this for a visual direction that other people must carry across a set: a portrait series, a launch suite, or a run of editorial covers. Creative direction decides what the work should say. Art direction decides what makes these particular images and treatments belong together.

## Inputs

Start with the project’s visual language, the placement list, available assets and their usage rights, and the time and budget for commissions. Note where a reference illustrates a technique rather than supplying usable artwork. If the asset library is incomplete, build a treatment study with available material and label the missing placements.

## Decisions & process

Choose what holds the set together. A constant camera distance and lighting direction can make different subjects feel equally important; you give up some freedom to suit each sitter. If character matters more, vary the framing and let background treatment or tonal range carry the family resemblance. Lay out three unlike subjects together before settling on the invariant.

Annotate references by intent: borrow the hard side light from one and the subject-to-background separation from another. A single close reference communicates faster, but can make the team copy irrelevant details. Several specific references let collaborators adapt the treatment, at the cost of spelling out which cue wins when they conflict.

Assign assets by role. A distinctive hero can set the tone, while quieter secondary images keep a series from peaking on every page. If each placement will mostly be seen alone, give secondary images more presence even if a wall of them feels busier.

## Typical mistakes & examples

A board of warm portraits gives photographers a color mood but no framing rule. The portraits look related in the deck yet alternate between tiny faces and tight headshots in a speaker grid. Show the grid with a common head scale and mark where an intentional exception belongs.

## Deliverables & questions for the human

Show an annotated reference sheet, selected assets in representative placements, and one set view with the fixed and flexible cues marked. Ask: Should viewers recognize the series first or notice each subject’s individuality first? Which placements warrant a commission rather than an adaptation?
