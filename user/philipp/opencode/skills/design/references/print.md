# Print

## When to load & job

Use this for artwork made to be trimmed, folded, or bound: a poster, postcard, or folded leaflet. Work out what should reach the edge, what must survive handling and trimming, and what the printer needs to make a proof. Use Editorial when the sequence of pages is the main design problem.

## Inputs

Start with the copy, source images, brand assets, and how the piece will be held or displayed. Take trim size, bleed, safe area, folds, color profile, stock, and export settings from the printer’s production specification. Without it, make a layout study with the assumed format marked; leave production export and paper-dependent color decisions open.

## Decisions & process

- **Bleed or border.** Let an image run off the sheet when the crop should feel continuous. Extend the image beyond trim by the specified bleed, and keep faces or other critical details inside the safe area. A deliberate white border preserves the whole picture, but a narrow border makes ordinary trim variation more visible. Show the same crop at trim size both ways.
- **Fold or uninterrupted surface.** Give a headline or contact block one panel when it needs to read without opening the piece. Carry a background across the fold if the opened spread needs to feel continuous, at the cost of an image that may look incomplete when closed. Show both states.
- **Ink on the specified stock.** If shadow detail carries the image, offer a lighter treatment alongside the denser original in a proof request. Opening up the shadows may separate tones on uncoated stock, but it can soften the atmosphere. A screen preview cannot settle that choice.

## Typical mistakes & examples

Ending a dark background at the trim line leaves no image where the cut drifts outward; a white sliver appears on the finished edge. Extend the background into the specified bleed rather than enlarging the whole layout and pushing captions toward trim.

## Deliverables & questions for the human

Show the artwork at trim size, an annotated flat, and a proof request naming the stock and areas to compare. Ask what must remain visible when folded. The printer supplies the physical proof; production approval belongs to the human and printer.
