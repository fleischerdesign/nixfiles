# UX (Usage Paths)

## When to load & job

Use this for usage paths: how people get from a starting situation to an outcome. Experience is an alias for usage paths. Work on sequence, friction, and recovery rather than visual styling. Bring in Interaction when the open question is how a particular control responds.

## Inputs

Start with the person's goal, what they already know or have at hand, and the conditions that can interrupt them. Read existing flows, support reports, and research if available; get policy constraints and handoff rules from their owners. Without that evidence, show a proposed flow with explicit assumptions rather than describing it as observed behavior. Leave policy-dependent branches open.

## Decisions & process

Choose how much the person has to decide at once. For a reimbursement with unfamiliar eligibility rules, ask the question that changes which evidence is needed before collecting receipts. For repeat submissions with predictable requirements, put the relevant fields together so people can enter them in one pass. A guided sequence reduces simultaneous choices but can make backtracking slower.

Decide which friction earns its place. Let people revise reversible choices as they go. Put a separate review step before a consequential submission if it lets them catch an error they cannot fix afterward. That pause costs time, so show the exact consequence it helps them understand.

Plan re-entry as part of the path. If a missing document can be supplied later, offer a route back to the unfinished step rather than another trip through intake. Saving a draft reduces repeated work but requires a clear answer about retention and shared devices.

## Typical mistakes & examples

Treating eligibility as the last step makes someone complete an application they cannot submit. Bring the decisive eligibility question forward; if the answer is uncertain, show the route to clarification without asking for the full application.

## Deliverables & questions for the human

Show the task flow from entry to outcome, including one realistic interruption and return. Name what each person or service needs at handoff. Ask which decisions are reversible and what people are likely to have available when they start.
