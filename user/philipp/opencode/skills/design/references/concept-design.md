# Concept Design

## When to load & job

Use this for sketches, scenario boards, and visual development that help a team decide what to make before it commits to production. A concept should make a proposed object, setting, or behavior tangible enough to discuss. It does not settle engineering, construction, or final asset detail.

## Inputs

Bring the project premise, the people or characters involved, the intended use or story beats, and any known constraints on setting, scale, and movement. Use supplied dimensions or requirements where they exist. If they don’t, draw relative scale and label what you assumed; leave fitted geometry and production details to the relevant specialist.

## Decisions & process

Choose the first view by the uncertainty. If recognition is open, compare silhouettes at the scale and angle where the audience first sees them. That hides useful detail but exposes ideas that only differ in ornament. If interaction is open, start with three frames showing approach, use, and departure; you give up polish to show how the concept changes over time.

Decide what to carry from roughs into a developed view. A cutaway is useful when the premise depends on an interior and exterior fitting together; a surface study is better when material or lighting is the open question. Do not let finish stand in for decisions about volume. Reuse the same human-scale figure or neighboring object across variants so the comparison means something.

## Typical mistakes & examples

A painted observatory interior can make a rotating telescope feel plausible while concealing where the operator stands as it moves. Show two positions of the telescope in the same section, with the operator and their route drawn in. Keep the paint study for the light and atmosphere question.

## Deliverables & questions for the human

Show competing roughs, the chosen concept in a short scenario, and annotated views that separate intent from assumptions. Ask which story beat must remain clear if the form changes. Name who takes the next step: for a set, the production designer or technical lead develops a dimensioned layout before construction decisions.
