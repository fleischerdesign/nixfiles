# VFX

## When to load & job

Use this for an effects shot’s previz, look development, or compositing plan. Decide what belongs in the photographed scene, what must be added or removed, and what will make those layers feel like one shot. This is not final compositing.

## Inputs

Bring the edit, shot IDs, source plates, and available lens and camera notes. Ask the production or post team for authoritative frame ranges, handles, and color transforms. Without the plate or metadata, use a marked proxy to show placement, depth, and timing. Leave precise tracking and color matching unresolved.

## Decisions & process

- **Follow a plane or solve the camera.** A planar track can anchor signage to a wall without a full scene solve. If the camera move reveals depth between elements, mock up separate depths and plan a camera track. That takes more setup, but avoids forcing objects at different distances to move as one surface.
- **Use the plate or replace it.** Keep the photographed foreground where it supplies useful contact, occlusion, and lighting cues. Replace a larger area when seams cross moving detail or the plate cannot support the look. You gain control but inherit the job of rebuilding those cues.
- **Integrate or deliberately separate.** For an effect that belongs in the room, plan its spill, shadow, and edge behavior from the plate. For a deliberately graphic effect, isolate it instead; it can stay clear without convincing the viewer that it lights the set.

## Typical mistakes & examples

A window replacement looks stable until an actor crosses, then the new view covers wisps of hair the glass should sit behind. Plan the foreground holdout separately from the window track and show the overlap on the crossing frames, not just on a clean still.

## Deliverables & questions for the human

Show a beat in the cut, annotated start and overlap frames, and a layer plan naming tracks, mattes, and plate dependencies. Ask whether the effect should be noticed or accepted as part of the scene. The compositor’s next proof is a moving shot study using the actual plate and agreed color pipeline.
