# Environmental Graphics

## When to load & job

Use this for graphics that become part of a place: entrance identities, wall graphics, room signs, or interpretive panels. Work out what the graphic should do at each viewing distance and how it meets the building. If the open question is which turn a visitor takes next, let wayfinding lead.

## Inputs

Start with the project's visual system, site photos from actual approaches, elevations, and the site's cleaning and replacement practices. Ask facilities for measured dimensions, surface restrictions, and finish information. Without those, make a photo study and label the assumed viewpoint, scale, and substrate; leave dimensions and material selection open.

## Decisions & process

- If the entrance needs to read across a forecourt, give the name one clear gesture and reserve close reading for a second surface. If the audience already pauses at the door, bring more of the story into that encounter, at the cost of a simpler distant silhouette. Show both approach views.
- If messages change with tenants or programs, study a sign face that can be replaced without redoing the wall. If the identity is stable and the architecture should carry it, study an integrated graphic instead; revisions would disturb more of the surface.
- Compare matte and reflective finishes under the site's likely daylight and evening conditions. A reflective face may suit the architecture from one approach and lose contrast from another. Show the intended character in context, then ask for physical samples rather than trusting the render.

## Typical mistakes & examples

A mural aligned on a flat elevation runs lettering across a recessed joint → from the entry, the recess hides part of the name → move the wordmark onto one continuous plane and let only the background cross the joint.

## Deliverables & questions for the human

Show annotated photo overlays, sign-face prototypes, and candidate finishes with assumptions. The site team supplies measured conditions, and a signage specialist provides an in-place mockup and samples. Final placement, material specification, and site approval remain outside the study. Which messages must be replaceable, and which surfaces must remain untouched?
