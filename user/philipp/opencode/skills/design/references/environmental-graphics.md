# Environmental Graphics

## When to load & job

Use this for graphics that become part of a place: entrance identities, donor walls, room identifiers, and interpretive panels. Make a site study that explains what people are meant to recognize, read, or linger over and how the graphic meets the building. If the unresolved question is which turn a visitor should take, let wayfinding lead and bring this module in for the physical sign family. A study is a brief for the site team and fabricator, not a fabrication drawing.

## Inputs & concepts

Collect the current identity assets and names, site photographs along actual approaches, elevations and sections, the message inventory, and expected update cycles. Ask facilities for measured surfaces, recesses, joints, finishes, restrictions on penetrations or adhesives, and cleaning and replacement practices. Ask the operator when people queue or when a surface is obscured by an open door. Without measured conditions, make a photo study with assumed viewpoint, scale, and substrate explicitly labeled.

A **viewing episode** combines an approach, angle, task, and available attention: crossing a forecourt and waiting at a door are different episodes even on the same façade. An **artwork elevation** locates content on surfaces; a perspective overlay shows what those surfaces reveal or hide. A **change boundary** separates what stays installed from what can be replaced. It may coincide with a panel edge but need not coincide with the artwork's visual edge. A joint or reveal is a physical interruption, not a line that disappears because the print file spans it.

## Decisions & procedure

- Give the entrance name one clear gesture when people must recognize it across a forecourt. Put interpretation closer to the point of pause. Combining the two saves a surface but costs distant simplicity; it makes more sense when most visitors already stop at the door.
- An integrated graphic can borrow the scale and rhythm of the architecture; a replaceable face limits the work and disruption of a tenant change. A distinct insert may introduce seams or hardware that the integrated treatment avoids.
- Matte and reflective faces can belong to different architectural expressions. Reflections can mask a face from one approach while making it prominent from another. Use renders to compare placement, then brief real finish samples under the site's daylight and evening conditions.

1. Assign each surface a communication job and identify what must still read with color or secondary marks absent. Where institutional and tenant identities compete, use [Identity systems](knowledge/identity-systems.md) (`references/knowledge/identity-systems.md`) to assign recognition roles. Where a surface carries directions, take its message and approach from [Wayfinding](knowledge/wayfinding.md) (`references/knowledge/wayfinding.md`) rather than writing new destination names into the artwork.
2. Build a **surface-and-view register**: surface ID, message, viewing episode, physical interruptions, expected change, responsible owner, and evidence still needed. Draw the same IDs on the elevation and annotated approach photos. Separate artwork extents from the building planes and mark candidate carrier boundaries so they can be discussed without implying a fixing detail.
3. Compare an integrated study with a replaceable one on the same views. Use a named close view to expose joint alignment and a moving approach view to expose foreshortening or occlusion. Carry the chosen hierarchy into sign-face prototypes. For a printed face, use [Print PDF](knowledge/print-pdf.md) (`references/knowledge/print-pdf.md`) to derive exchange geometry once the fabricator supplies it.

## Worked example & typical mistakes

A museum wants its name above the entrance and an exhibition subtitle that changes seasonally. The frontal elevation makes a recessed door bay look like part of the main wall. The study register records the forecourt approach as oblique from the left and the subtitle encounter as a pause alongside the entrance. On the perspective overlay, the near return of the recess hides the last letters of the name. Moving the entire name onto one continuous plane keeps it together in both views; only the background band crosses the recess. The changing subtitle goes on a separate close-reading face. The typical mistake is aligning a continuous wordmark to the elevation's overall centerline: the graphic is centered in the drawing but incomplete at the approach. Move the wordmark before refining its scale or finish.

## Standards, limits & handover

For a U.S. project, the DOJ *2010 ADA Standards for Accessible Design* §216 describes which signs are scoped; §703 gives technical provisions. `verify` applicability with the project specialist, especially when an identity treatment shares a location with required identification. ISO 15930-7:2010 is a PDF/X exchange standard, not a sign substrate or mounting specification; `verify` relevant clauses if the supplier requests it. Face dimensions, cut details, fastening, and finishes come from the project and fabricator. Register IDs are a drawing convention; the museum is illustrative.

Show the human the paired approach overlays, annotated elevation, sign-face studies, and finish sample brief. Facilities supplies measured conditions; the signage specialist develops an in-place mockup and installation detail. Final placement and materials remain open. Ask which messages must change independently and which surfaces must remain untouched.
