# Packaging

## When to load & job

Use this for artwork on a carton, label, sleeve, or pouch. Design for two moments: choosing the right pack in a row, then turning it to find the details. Construction and material approval are outside this job.

## Inputs

Get the shelf or listing context, SKU matrix, brand assets, and mandatory copy by market from the product and regulatory owners. Use the converter’s issued dieline and print specification for panel orientation, folds, seams, glue areas, bleed, safe areas, and color profile. If the dieline is missing, make a panel study with the assumed geometry labeled; don’t build production artwork on it.

## Decisions & process

- **Family first or variant first.** Let a recognizable product image lead when shoppers are choosing between different kinds of product. When they’re distinguishing similar flavors or strengths, give the variant name a fixed place on the front and let imagery support it. That leaves less room for a hero crop. Put the closest look-alikes next to each other at shelf scale, not on separate slides.
- **One face or a wrap.** Let a background run across an edge if continuity helps the pack read as one object. Keep wording that must read in one glance on a single face, even if that leaves less room for illustration. Rotate a folded mockup through the faces shoppers will actually see.
- **Copy sets the panel budget.** If one pack must serve several markets, reserve panel space using the supplied copy before sizing the illustration. Market-specific artwork can free up space, but adds versions to maintain. Lay out the most copy-heavy variant beside the shortest one and keep the barcode’s specified clear space visible on the flat.

## Typical mistakes & examples

Centering an ingredient statement across two panels looks balanced on the flat; after folding, part of the sentence disappears around the corner. Give the statement one uninterrupted face and let the background cross the fold instead.

## Deliverables & questions for the human

Show a shelf lineup, annotated flat, and folded preview. Ask which two variants shoppers most need to tell apart, and which face the shelf presents. Request a physical proof from the converter and send copy questions to the regulatory owner; production decisions wait for their input.
