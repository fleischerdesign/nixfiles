# Sound

## When to load & job

Use this for a sound direction brief or for reviewing cues against a cut or interaction recording. Describe what the listener should notice, anticipate, or feel, and what a cue tells them about the action. Let film and video lead if the shot sequence is still the question; let interaction lead if the underlying outcome or feedback state is undecided. A cue sheet and a marked reference playback are design artifacts; recording, synthesis, editing, and the final mix belong to the audio team.

## Inputs & concepts

Bring a versioned cut or interaction recording, dialogue and music references, and intended playback contexts: headphones, phone speaker, quiet room, or busy public space. Editorial supplies the picture timebase; engineering supplies event meanings and what can overlap or repeat. The destination owner supplies the loudness and peak specification. Without one, mark delivery levels as unset and describe what should be heard in front or behind rather than inventing targets.

A cue's **anchor** is the picture moment or event it relates to, not necessarily the instant its audio starts. The **attack** is the opening of the sound; its most recognizable instant may occur after the onset. The **tail** can extend into dialogue or a new interaction. **Masking** occurs when another sound makes a cue harder to hear; sharing a register or transient is one reason it happens. The **bed** is the continuing background under the foreground event; a music drop can leave room tone intact. Separate all of these listening relationships from measured programme loudness and true peak, which describe different aspects of a deliverable.

## Decisions & procedure

- **Sync or anticipate.** Anchor confirmation to a completed action when sound implies cause. Lead a known picture reveal for suspense; leading an uncertain outcome falsely confirms it.
- **Use contrast or sustain a bed.** Pull music back if a line should feel exposed. Retain room tone if total silence would sound like an accidental dropout. Name the layer that stays.
- **Carry detail or carry through.** Soft texture can reward close listening but vanish on a phone in noise. Try a distinct attack or uncrowded register before requesting more level; this may feel insistent in a quiet room.

1. Mark the spoken lines, gestures, or outcomes that already explain the action. Write what each added sound contributes beyond that evidence: orientation, anticipation, confirmation, or atmosphere. Remove cues whose only job is to fill space.
2. Make a **spotting sheet** against the versioned source. Use [Sound cues](knowledge/sound-cues.md) (`references/knowledge/sound-cues.md`) for translating anchors and competing layers into cue rows. Add a reference excerpt or spoken description of the onset and tail if a label such as “chime” could mean very different sounds. State which feature of the reference matters.
3. Present a beat with its neighboring events, not a solo cue. Where two layers compete, brief the team on which layer can yield and which carries information. For interactions, include muted and repeated use: retain essential information visually, and reserve the strongest motif for the moment it actually distinguishes.

## Worked example & typical mistakes

In a documentary, a catch clicks shut just as someone says the name of the next stop. The click must locate the action, but its bright tail competes with the name. Keep the transient on the contact frame and request a shorter tail; ask for music to recede through the line while room tone continues. Sliding the whole click later to clear the voice would detach the sound from its visible cause. In an app, the analogous hierarchy error is using the same sharp chime on every form step: progress sounds final each time, and the actual completion has no distinctive cue. Leave routine steps visually legible and save the motif for completion.

## Standards, limits & handover

[ITU-R BS.1770-5](https://www.itu.int/rec/R-REC-BS.1770/en) (11/2023), Annexes 1 and 2, defines algorithms for programme loudness and guidance for true-peak metering, respectively. [EBU R 128](https://tech.ebu.ch/publications/r128) is a broadcast recommendation; `verify` its adopted revision and any distributor requirements rather than treating it as an app default. “Voice in front of music” is a listening direction, not a target measurement.

Show the spotting sheet beside playback with intentional gaps marked. Ask which cue must carry information and which should disappear on repeat use. The sound designer supplies a temp track against real picture or interaction; the mixer needs the destination's specification and the intended playback contexts.
