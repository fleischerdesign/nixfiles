# Visual Design

## When to load & job

Use this when the same content or identity must work as a visual system across formats. Decide what the eye finds first, how the pieces relate, and which relationships survive a change of medium. Art direction may set the image treatment; here those images have to share space with type, color, and real content.

## Inputs

Bring the message priorities, real copy and assets, existing type and color roles, and the formats people will encounter. Use dimensions and output constraints supplied by each medium’s owner. If those are missing, set up representative studies and label the assumed sizes instead of presenting output files as final.

## Decisions & process

Build a reading order before choosing a composition. A large image can earn the first glance on a poster where the event needs recognition; on a schedule card, a prominent date and venue help people act. You give up identical silhouettes across the set. Put both pieces next to each other and mark what should be read first and second.

Carry type roles across media, not literal sizes. A display face can announce a title at a distance; on a narrow card with a long title, a less wide face from the existing system may keep the name together. Keep the relationship to the supporting copy, even if the scale changes. Show the longest real title alongside a short one.

Choose what color does. A broad accent field can tie standalone pieces together; reserving the accent for the next action can make a dense composition easier to scan. Show a pair in context to make the loss of color presence or emphasis visible.

## Typical mistakes & examples

Shrinking a print poster verbatim into a narrow event tile preserves its margins and proportions but reduces the date to a footnote. Recompose around the date and title, then carry the alignment and type relationships rather than the original coordinates.

## Deliverables & questions for the human

Show representative layouts with real content at their intended viewing sizes and a small sheet of shared roles and format-specific changes. Ask: Which information must survive when space collapses? Which format should carry the strongest expression of the identity?
