# Business Design

## When to load & job

Load this for a value model, commercial model comparison, or partner proposition. Make visible who benefits, who pays, who does the work, and who carries the risk. Strategic design may choose which opportunity to pursue; business design explains how the exchange could sustain it.

## Inputs

Bring the offer, customer and payer roles, contractual constraints, existing revenue and cost data, and any research on buying behavior. Separate actual terms and figures from assumptions. If figures are missing, show the economics as a formula with named inputs; leave margins and forecasts unstated.

## Decisions & process

Separate the buyer from the beneficiary where they differ. Selling to an employer may remove a price barrier for staff, but introduces procurement and renewal decisions that the end user cannot make. Selling directly gives users the choice but makes them bear the cost. Draw separate flows for payment, use, and benefit.

Choose a charging unit that follows value without distorting behavior. Per-use pricing lowers the commitment for occasional customers but may discourage beneficial use. A subscription smooths the bill but asks infrequent customers to carry a fixed cost. Show the same customer scenarios under both models.

Decide which risks stay with the business and which go to partners. A fixed referral fee makes partner income predictable per referral; paying on completed work ties compensation to a later outcome but shifts risk onto a partner who may not control fulfillment. Put payment triggers next to the actions each party controls.

## Typical mistakes & examples

Counting marketplace fees without including disputes and rework makes every transaction look alike. A model based on that average can favor volume even when complex jobs drive support costs. Trace a routine job and a disputed job separately, with unknown cost drivers left open.

## Deliverables & questions for the human

Show the value flows, competing models, and the assumptions that would change their relative merits. Customer research can probe willingness to pay; finance needs actual costs and transaction data before drawing conclusions about viability. Ask: Who has budget authority? Who bears refunds? Which party can reduce the cost we are asking it to absorb?
