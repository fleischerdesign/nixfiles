# Business Design

## When to load & job

Load this for a value model, commercial model comparison, or partner proposition. The brief makes visible who benefits, pays, performs the work, and carries uncertainty. Strategic design asks which opportunity deserves focus; business design asks how the exchange could fund and sustain the work. Service design takes the operating episode further when delivery across roles is the main question. The output is a conditional model and review framework, not a valuation, demand forecast, or conclusion about viability.

## Inputs & concepts

Get the offer and commercial objective from the sponsor; buyer, payer and beneficiary roles from sales and research; actual price, discounts, refund terms and settlement timing from commercial or contract owners; delivery effort and exceptions from operations; and cost definitions from finance. Record which terms are in force, proposed, or unknown. When volumes or costs are missing, keep them as named variables. Do not substitute price-list values for revenue retained, interview enthusiasm for demand, or contract signature for cash received.

The **charging unit** is what triggers a bill; the **comparison unit** is what you hold equivalent across options, such as serving one participating employer over a renewal period. They need not match. **Contribution** here means modeled retained revenue less the costs finance treats as variable for the stated scope; it is not profit. Set fixed commitments and timing of receipts and payments alongside it. **Risk allocation** says who absorbs an unfavorable outcome; **control** says who can prevent or reduce it. The two can sit with different actors, changing the incentives to participate.

## Decisions & procedure

- **Direct purchaser versus sponsor.** Direct purchase gives the beneficiary discretion but leaves them with the cost. An employer or institution may remove that price barrier while adding procurement, eligibility and renewal decisions the user cannot control.
- **Usage fee versus recurring fee.** A usage charge can suit occasional purchasers but discourage beneficial use. A recurring fee can smooth bills while asking infrequent users to pay in periods with little benefit. Compare both over the same scope, not a subscription period against one favorable transaction.
- **Pay for initiation versus completion.** Paying a partner for a referral rewards a step they control but leaves fulfillment risk with the business. Paying on completion can align with delivery while transferring risk to a partner with little influence over downstream capacity.

1. Draw distinct flows for money, work and benefit between named actors. On each payment arrow put the billable event, who confirms it, and who can reverse it. For comparing alternative commercial arrangements without confusing their commitments, load [Option framing](knowledge/option-framing.md) (`references/knowledge/option-framing.md`).
2. Build a **transaction-and-exposure ledger** for a routine case and a material exception. For each, show event, responsible actor, cost driver, payment or refund trigger and timing, and who has discretion. If a charge depends on something hard to observe across a service handoff, load [Service blueprint](knowledge/service-blueprint.md) (`references/knowledge/service-blueprint.md`) to locate the operating evidence and the owner of the exception.
3. Translate the ledger into named terms: retained revenue minus variable delivery, partner and support costs, using finance's definitions. Keep refunded amounts from being counted twice. Show fixed commitments separately. Compare ordinary and exception cases first; aggregate only if supported case mix and volumes are supplied. Keep payment timing visible even where contribution is unchanged.
4. Ask how the offer changes behavior after launch. Where incentives could alter use, rework or capacity, load [Feedback loops](knowledge/feedback-loops.md) (`references/knowledge/feedback-loops.md`) to trace the return. Brief the sponsor on the commercial terms and evidence that could change the preference, not a margin extrapolated from a convenient transaction.

## Worked example & typical mistakes

In an illustrative repair marketplace, the options are paying a referrer when a slot is booked or after the customer accepts completion. A customer disputes a repair after a second visit. The ledger shows that the completion trigger now depends on how the marketplace resolves the dispute; the referrer cannot change either repair quality or support timing. Under booking-based pay the referral cost remains even if the marketplace refunds all its fee. Under completion-based pay the marketplace may avoid that payment in this branch, but it still owes whatever support and rework costs its terms assign to it. The useful question is whether the referrer can influence job suitability before booking, not whether a later trigger looks cheaper on this row.

Counting fee income on every booking makes a volume increase look attractive while hiding refunds and case work. Put disputed cases beside routine ones and leave their frequency unknown until operations can supply it.

## Standards, limits & handover

Jean-Charles Rochet and Jean Tirole, “Platform Competition in Two-Sided Markets,” *Journal of the European Economic Association* 1(4), 2003, pp. 990–1029, models why price structure across sides matters; it is not a norm or a pricing recommendation. Contracts and finance definitions are project inputs; applicable accounting and consumer requirements depend on the actual offer and jurisdiction (`verify` with finance or counsel). Ledger columns are conventions, and the referral triggers are examples. Use a spreadsheet with separate source terms and formulas so finance can inspect the assumed scope.

Show the flows, ledger, options and pivotal unknowns. Research can investigate buying behavior; procurement can name budget authority; finance needs actual transactions and costs before drawing conclusions. Ask who can reduce the cost they would absorb, who bears refunds, and who controls the budget.
