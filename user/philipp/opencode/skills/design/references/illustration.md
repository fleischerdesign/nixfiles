# Illustration

## When to load & job

Use this for an image that depicts a scene, explains an idea through metaphor, or carries a story alongside the copy. Decide what the image adds that the words don't. If the asset has to identify an interface action at a glance, let iconography lead instead.

## Inputs

Bring the actual copy or storyboard, layout and crop slots, brand references, and source material for any real subject. Work from supplied or licensed assets, or draw original artwork; don't invent a product feature or depict a stand-in as the actual object. If output specs are missing, show a composition study at stated dimensions and leave final exports until the production owner supplies them.

## Decisions & process

- **Choose literal depiction or metaphor.** If readers must recognize a particular subject, work from references and preserve the distinguishing features. If the piece is about an abstract relationship, sketch a metaphor that expresses the relationship through the action in the scene. You gain room for interpretation but give up literal specificity.
- **Compose for the placement.** A single flexible composition can serve several sizes when the focal action and the text reserve survive each crop. If a narrow slot would cut away half of an interaction, redraw it for that slot. A second composition costs time but preserves the story.
- **Choose detail for the reproduction method.** Texture can set the mood on a large screen or spread. At small sizes or with limited inks, distinct silhouettes and separated shapes carry more of the image. Recompose important detail into larger shapes rather than just removing texture from the same drawing.

## Typical mistakes & examples

A story about sharing responsibility gets a decorative image of two people beside a giant puzzle piece. The piece doesn't show what they do for each other. Show them passing a load from one set of hands to another, and keep the handoff inside both crops. Let the action, not the prop, carry the idea.

## Deliverables & questions for the human

Show two rough compositions beside the real copy, then the chosen artwork in the intended crops and smallest placement. Identify any source assets and unresolved usage rights. Ask: Is the image meant to depict a specific real subject, or can it interpret the idea? Which placement must retain the full scene?
