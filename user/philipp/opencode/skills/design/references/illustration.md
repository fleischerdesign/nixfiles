# Illustration

## When to load & job

Use this for an image that depicts a scene, explains an idea through metaphor, or carries a story alongside the copy. Decide what the image adds that the words don't. If an asset must identify an interface action at a glance, let iconography lead. If the job is to lay down the visual direction for a family of images rather than make a particular picture, let art direction lead. Deliver layered artwork where the medium is known, or a composition study and artist brief where it isn't.

## Inputs & concepts

Bring the real copy or storyboard, layout and crop slots, brand references, and subject material. Ask the editor which ideas the copy already handles and the subject owner which features need factual fidelity. Get reproduction dimensions, file format, and any color or ink constraints from the production owner. Record which references are for observation only and which assets are licensed or supplied for reuse. If output specs are missing, show a study at stated dimensions and leave final exports open.

The **story beat** is the moment the reader should see: who acts, on what, and what visibly changes. The **focal action** is the part of the drawing that carries that beat; it can require several figures or objects, not just one focal point. A **value study** groups light and dark before detail so the important shapes don't merge. The **text reserve** is room for actual copy where copy and art share a frame; don't count space the layout will cover as available to the story.

## Decisions & procedure

- **Depict the object or interpret the relationship.** Preserve distinguishing features where the reader has to recognize a particular object; a stand-in can falsely represent a product feature. For an abstraction, use a metaphor carried by an action, not a decorative prop. You gain room for interpretation and give up specificity.
- **Crop or recompose.** Reuse one drawing if its essential action and room for type survive all placements. Redraw a narrow or small variant when cropping removes part of the story. This adds work but protects meaning instead of just protecting the protagonist.
- **Choose detail for reproduction.** Texture can set the tone on a spread. At small sizes or with limited inks, combine secondary detail into fewer masses and draw the information-bearing shapes larger. That changes the composition, not just the surface treatment.

1. Write the beat as a verb and identify the visual evidence for it. Separate factual features that must be drawn from choices the illustrator can interpret. If the brief calls for invention, name what the invented scene must not imply.
2. Make a **thumbnail and value sheet** beside the real copy. Draw two structurally different framings or moments, such as a wide setup versus a close interaction. Annotate what establishes the setting and what shows the beat. When choosing reference frames or planning how an image will occupy delivery slots, use [Image selection](knowledge/image-selection.md) (`references/knowledge/image-selection.md`) for framing and crop decisions.
3. Draw the chosen composition with figure, ground, copy reserve, and texture on separable layers where the medium permits. Build a recomposed variant around the same beat if needed, then place both with type at their real sizes. Keep the drawing source distinct from the flattened or cropped derivatives.

## Worked example & typical mistakes

An article about shared responsibility needs a wide header and a narrow card. On the value sheet, the wide version has two figures separated by a gap, one offering a crate and the other reaching to support it. The story needs both sets of hands and the crate between them; the feet only establish the setting. In the card, instead of cropping until one figure disappears, redraw closer from a slight angle. One dark sleeve enters from above, the other from below, with both hands clearly overlapping the light crate. Leave the moment of exchange clear of the title. The wide image shows who is involved; the card retains what they do for one another.

A giant puzzle piece beside two figures names a theme without depicting any exchange. The reader sees an emblem, not a relationship. Put the meaning in what the figures are doing.

## Standards, limits & handover

For web vector output, the W3C [SVG 2](https://www.w3.org/TR/2018/CR-SVG2-20181004/coords.html) Candidate Recommendation (4 October 2018), §§8.2 and 8.6, explains viewport mapping and `viewBox`; scaling the viewport won't recompose an illustration. For reused assets under [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/legalcode.en), §3(a) sets attribution conditions, including indicating modifications when sharing the licensed material. Apply the actual license, not this example license by analogy. File formats and permitted inks come from the project or supplier. Value grouping is a drawing convention, not a reproduction requirement.

Show both roughs next to their copy and the chosen artwork at each intended crop, including the smallest placement. Identify references, reused assets, and any rights still with their owners. Ask whether the subject must be depicted faithfully and which placement needs the full scene rather than only the beat.
