# Speculative & Critical Design

## When to load & job

Use this for a scenario and an artifact that invite people to question a present assumption. Unlike a roadmap, the work doesn't recommend what to build next; unlike a forecast, it doesn't say what will happen. State what the work is arguing before choosing its form.

## Inputs

Start with the practice or belief under question, the audience, where they'll encounter the work, and documented conditions that anchor the premise. Separate what is known from what the scenario invents. If the audience or venue is undecided, script one assumed encounter and leave its reception open.

## Decisions & process

- Pick the distance from the present. Change one rule in a familiar setting if you want people to trace its consequences. A more distant world can question assumptions that feel fixed today, but requires more exposition. On a scenario card, name what changed and what stayed ordinary.
- Choose an artifact someone would encounter inside that world: a receipt, appeal letter, or interface with a consequential default. A familiar format can make the premise tangible but risks being mistaken for a real service. An overtly fictional frame reduces confusion, though it can soften the initial surprise.
- Decide how much the artifact should say outright. A clear contradiction helps a short encounter land; an unresolved choice gives a facilitated audience room to disagree but can obscure the argument without a discussion prompt.

## Typical mistakes & examples

A glossy demo for a city that sells quiet street hours can read as a pitch, so the audience debates pricing instead of who controls public space. Add a denied appeal from a night-shift worker and frame both artifacts as fiction; the encounter now exposes who can contest the rule.

## Deliverables & questions for the human

Show the scenario card, prototype in context, argument, and prompts for discussion. A facilitator needs to hear how actual audiences interpret the encounter; a domain specialist can challenge its grounding. Neither makes it a prediction. What assumption should be hardest to ignore? How much ambiguity can the venue support?
