# 3D

## When to load & job

Use this for a code-based scene, object study, or interactive spatial prototype. Decide what geometry, camera, light, and material need to communicate. The prototype can convey form and behavior; it is not a production asset or a substitute for a final render pipeline.

## Inputs

Bring reference views, known dimensions and units, target framing or interaction, and any supplied assets. Take asset formats and rendering constraints from the runtime or production team. If dimensions are unknown, label relative scale and use proxy geometry; fit and exact material appearance remain unresolved.

## Decisions & process

- **Model the silhouette or suggest the surface.** Spend geometry on profiles, openings, and edges that must cast shadows or hold up in an orbit. Use textures or normal maps for fine relief at the intended distance. That keeps the prototype lighter, but the relief disappears at a grazing silhouette.
- **Explain shape or stage depth.** An orthographic view keeps parallel edges parallel and makes proportions easier to compare. A perspective camera shows how parts overlap from a viewer’s position, but proximity can exaggerate one part. Put both views beside each other before interpreting scale.
- **Show form or show finish.** Start with a clay material and a broad side light when volume is uncertain. Once the shape reads, introduce the intended material and give reflective surfaces something to reflect. A dramatic key can sell a finish from one angle while hiding the profile from another.

## Typical mistakes & examples

A chamfer on a glossy housing is too small to catch a highlight at the chosen camera distance. Raising the key light brightens the entire face without making the edge readable. Try a broader reflection or adjust the bevel in the study, marking any departure from supplied dimensions.

## Deliverables & questions for the human

Show the working scene at its intended camera, with an orbit or second view and a clay/material comparison. Ask which surfaces need to hold up under close, interactive viewing. A 3D artist can build the next asset study from confirmed dimensions and material references.
