# Exhibition

## When to load & job

Use this for an exhibition narrative and layout: what visitors encounter first, what brings them closer, and what they carry into the next room. Interior leads when the brief is about how the venue works between shows rather than how this particular story unfolds.

## Inputs

Get the curatorial premise, object list, loan and conservation conditions, measured venue plan, expected modes of visiting, and media operating assumptions. Ask the registrar for object and lender constraints and venue staff for arrival and turnover patterns. If the loans or plan are unsettled, storyboard with labeled placeholders and leave object placement and case geometry open.

## Decisions & process

- Use a clear sequence when objects build an argument that depends on order. When visitors can enter midway or skip rooms, give each cluster an opening idea of its own; that makes it easier to browse but gives up some control over the reveal. Draw both a full visit and a short visit in the storyboard.
- Put a slow encounter beside, rather than across, the main route if visitors will stop for a film or a detailed object. A side pocket lets others continue, but it makes the encounter less prominent. Sketch the standing audience and the view of the object from behind it.
- Choose media by what it adds to the object. A projection can show a mechanism in motion; a still sequence lets visitors set their own pace and makes fewer demands on lighting and sound. Put the two treatments next to the actual object story before choosing.

## Typical mistakes & examples

A film is used as the introduction to a rare object, so visitors gather at the screen and mask the case behind them. Put the case on the initial sightline and move the film to an optional second encounter.

## Deliverables & questions for the human

Show a narrative storyboard, annotated layout, and a media and fabrication question list. Ask what visitors must encounter even on a short visit. The curator and conservator provide object requirements; the exhibition designer and fabricator provide full-scale viewing and display mock-ups. Case, mount, and rigging details and fabrication remain outside the study.
