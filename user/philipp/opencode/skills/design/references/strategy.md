# Strategic Design

## When to load & job

Load this for a decision brief about where to focus, what outcome to pursue, or what not to do. The job is to make competing directions discussable before anyone commits. Use business design instead when the open question is who pays or how the model sustains itself.

## Inputs

Start with the sponsor’s decision, who owns it, the time horizon, existing commitments, and any research or operating data supplied. Separate binding constraints from preferences. If the objective is contested, write alternative problem frames and name whose objective each serves. Don’t quietly choose one on the sponsor’s behalf.

## Decisions & process

Frame the decision around an outcome if the team is free to change the means. If a commitment fixes the means, narrow the frame to what can still change. Put the two frames side by side: the narrower one is easier to act on but can hide a better intervention outside its scope.

Compare genuinely different allocations of effort. If the team can reverse course cheaply, include a small commitment designed to resolve a pivotal uncertainty. If the opportunity depends on a fixed window, include a larger commitment and name what it displaces. Keep the current course in view so the alternatives have a meaningful baseline.

Agree on decision criteria before discussing favorites. Show where those criteria conflict rather than averaging them away: reach may favor one direction while time to deliver favors another.

## Typical mistakes & examples

Framing “launch a learning portal” as the goal makes every option a portal variant. If the intended outcome is faster onboarding, the team never gets to compare a portal with protected practice time. Reframe around the outcome and state what would have to be true for either direction to help.

## Deliverables & questions for the human

Show the decision brief with the options, opportunity costs, and assumptions that could reverse the choice. Researchers and operational owners supply evidence about needs and feasibility; the sponsor chooses whether to commit. Ask: Which constraint is binding? Which existing commitment can we stop? What uncertainty would change your decision?
