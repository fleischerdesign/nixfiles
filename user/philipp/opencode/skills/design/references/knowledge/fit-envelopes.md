# Fit envelopes

## Use when

A product appears to fit at rest but may interfere with a person, another object, or a service task in use. Produce coordinated plan and section overlays showing occupied, movement and service envelopes, with unresolved dimensions left as variables.

## Inputs

Gather a survey or dimensional drawing with units and reference points, relevant body measurements or task observations, expected posture and duration, clothing or aids, and neighboring objects in their operating positions. Ask the supplier for hinges, fasteners, removal directions, service tools and installation tolerances. Distinguish measured values, project requirements and illustrative proxies. If dimensions are missing, identify the datum and measurement the drawing needs rather than assigning a plausible clearance.

## Procedure

1. Establish shared datums, such as finished floor and rear mounting face. Draw the product and site from the same datums in plan and section. Identify protrusions and movable elements. Keep any unmeasured edge dashed and symbolic so the apparent scale does not turn into a specification.
2. Draw the **occupied envelope**: what is simultaneously present during a representative working posture or state, including the person, equipment, aids and adjacent users as relevant. Separate static body dimensions from task posture. Clearance may be driven by a large occupied form, while reach may be driven by a shorter reach from a constrained working position.
3. Draw the **movement envelope** from a sequence of poses and part positions: approach, reach or transfer, operation and withdrawal. Sweep knee, elbow, carried object, door or drawer through the part of the path that matters. Draw only activities that actually coincide on the same overlay, or label when a conflict is resolved by sequencing.
4. Draw the **service envelope** for installation, cleaning or replacement, including the operator, tools, disengagement motion and exit path of the part. Compare variants at the same task and state. Turn collisions into concrete measurement requests: name the two faces, the direction and the operating position to measure. Where use and service need not overlap, show the service position and who can make space for it.

## Limits

Three envelope names are drawing conventions. Anthropometric definitions are not population values or required clearances. Do not combine unrelated percentiles into a single “representative” person; use task-relevant evidence about the intended users. A fit study does not establish comfort, force, load capacity or code dimensions. Use the applicable primary norm or project and supplier data for exact requirements.

## Worked example

In an illustrative window bench, a cushion and base fit inside the wall recess. The section overlays an open lid and shows its front edge lifting through the path a seated person uses to rise. The plan also shows the removable storage bin must be pulled while a person stands in the aisle. A side-opening alternative removes the overhead sweep but competes with that aisle, so the study requests the measured aisle width with nearby furniture in use and the supplier's extraction travel. Neither sketch fixes the final bench depth.
