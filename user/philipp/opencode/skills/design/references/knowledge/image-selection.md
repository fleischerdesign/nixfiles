# Select images for roles and crop slots

## Use when

Choose frames for a campaign, story or interface, or explain which images need commissioning. Produce an annotated contact sheet, a crop sheet in context and a shot brief for empty slots.

## Inputs

Get the originals or contact sheet with asset IDs, captions and provenance, the communication job of each placement, foreground copy, and aspect ratios and output sizes. Ask the rights owner for permitted channels, territories, duration, edits, attribution and any relevant releases. Distinguish project assets from references that illustrate a treatment. If only previews exist, make a placement study and leave crop resolution and reuse rights open.

## Procedure

1. **Describe the job of each slot.** A hero introduces the subject, an evidence image demonstrates something, a detail explains a feature, and a supporting image may set context or pace. For each slot, write what the reader needs to see and what could be lost without changing the meaning. In a set, reserve wide context and close detail for different jobs instead of picking several frames that all say the same thing.
2. **Compare frames within their roles.** Lay out comparable candidates at the same thumbnail scale. Annotate gesture, gaze, context and relationships that must remain visible. Then view plausible frames at placement size beside typography and adjacent images. Distinguish the strongest full frame from the frame that still communicates after cropping.
3. **Draw crop windows on the original.** Mark each slot’s ratio, focal region, essential details and clear zone for copy or overlays. Move the image within the crop, not just the crop around the center. Record separate crops for genuinely different compositions; if no window preserves the relationship, select a different frame. Compute usable pixel dimensions from the remaining crop, not the whole source, and use the publisher’s requirements for scaling or alternate sources.
4. **Turn missing slots into a shot brief.** Specify the story function, people or objects, camera distance and angle, required gesture, protected framing, alternate orientation, overlay space and intended use. Include a rights line per selected asset with owner, applicable permissions and credit. If edits or reuse are unresolved, identify the picture as a placeholder rather than embedding it as a reusable asset.

## Limits

A contact sheet cannot establish authenticity, consent or permission. Rights and releases depend on the agreement and use; refer uncertainties to the rights owner or legal specialist. A tight crop can also change what a documentary image appears to say even when the edit is permitted.

## Worked example

An illustrative repair workshop feature needs a wide lead and a portrait card. The lead shows the worker, the clamp and the hand guiding a tool, with room at left for the title. A portrait crop of that frame keeps the worker but cuts the clamp, making the tool’s action ambiguous. Use a nearby vertical frame showing the hand, tool and clamp together. If none exists, brief a vertical detail that keeps all three in one plane and leaves clear space above for the card title, rather than asking for “a tighter crop.”
