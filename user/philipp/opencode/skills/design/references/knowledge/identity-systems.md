# Express identity relationships across touchpoints

## Use when

An organization, product family or program needs to be recognized across surfaces made by different teams. Produce a relationship map, a touchpoint matrix and a short set of usage rules, not just a sheet of marks.

## Inputs

Get the organization’s positioning, current names and marks, offer relationships, audience evidence, and where public recognition already lives. Collect actual touchpoints, including cramped, monochrome and partner-owned formats. Identify who owns each name, asset, channel and template. Where the relationship is unresolved, show competing structures as hypotheses rather than inferring recognition from the organization chart.

## Procedure

1. **Map what people need to recognize.** Put the parent, offers, divisions and partners on a map. Annotate where audiences need to identify the offer by itself, and where the parent supplies context or trust. Compare a parent-led signature with named offers, an offer-led signature with endorsement, and an independent offer where the relationship need not be visible. Use the same row of touchpoints for comparisons; a family masthead can obscure differences that become apparent in a directory or receipt.
2. **Assign fixed and flexible cues.** Pick the cues that carry recognition when others disappear: a name, signature, characteristic type relationship or alignment. Define where a compact or one-color signature replaces the full version. Give teams explicit variables for expression, such as an accent field, crop treatment or voice register. Do not make an accent color the only identifier where output may be monochrome or the hue already signals something else.
3. **Apply the rules where they are strained.** In the touchpoint matrix, list audience task, lead identity, secondary line, fixed cue, permitted variation, and exception. Populate the narrow header, monochrome notice and partner layout with real names and lengths. If an exception recurs, give it a defined variant rather than repeatedly improvising.
4. **Write guidelines as decisions people can reproduce.** Show an assembly diagram, linked source assets, and examples with the reason for each choice. Document when to choose each signature, where to put the endorsement or partner, what to do when space or color is limited, and who maintains the assets and resolves new relationships.

## Limits

A system study makes relationships visible; it does not establish audience recognition, naming rights or trademark clearance. The organization owns its offer relationships, and its rights team handles mark and name questions. A historical manual demonstrates one organization’s conventions, not a template for every identity.

## Worked example

An illustrative city library has distinct events and lending services. Annual reports lead with the library name, but a shared directory needs “Tool Lending” to stand out independently. Keep the library’s name as a quiet endorsement on that directory entry and in partner notices; use “Tool Lending” in the same lead type role online and on the physical door. Programs can vary their accent and images. In a black-and-white reservation slip the program is still named and visibly endorsed, so the family relationship does not rely on the accent.
