# Translate a dieline into packaging artwork

## Use when

Graphics and copy must wrap an issued carton, sleeve, label or pouch. Produce a numbered panel map, copy allocation, separation schedule and folded mockup that explain the same package from flat and assembled views.

## Inputs

Get the converter’s dieline revision, scale, units, print side, cut and crease definitions, bleed and exclusion areas, seam or glue instructions, stock and ink/finish schedule. Bring the SKU and market matrix, mandatory copy from its owner, barcode application and symbol artwork, and shelf or display orientation. If the structure is unsettled, make a labeled face study without treating its outlines as a converter dieline.

## Procedure

1. **Turn the dieline into a panel map.** Keep the supplied geometry unchanged on a separate layer. Assign IDs and draw a reading-top arrow on each visible face, including lid and base where relevant. Mark which faces touch, overlap or disappear under flaps. Assemble a blank paper dummy or use the converter’s folded reference before placing copy; on the flat, adjacent faces need not share a reading direction.
2. **Budget copy on visible faces.** Give every required block a panel, orientation and owner. Reserve area for the longest supplied language block and for the barcode with its required quiet zones. Select a barcode location for the actual application and scanning environment, considering seams, edges, curves, flaps and overwrap; use the applicable symbol and placement provisions rather than a copied package’s dimensions. Move artwork or use market-specific variants if the copy cannot fit the usable faces.
3. **Define what crosses each edge or prints separately.** Keep sentences, critical image features and scanner zones within usable faces. Let background cross a fold only where the folded result benefits; run it beyond a cut as specified and leave glue and seal areas according to the converter’s instructions. Map process inks, named spot inks and special masks such as white or varnish to separate deliverables or layers as agreed. List overprint or knockout behavior per supplier instructions and distinguish structural marks from printing separations.
4. **Pair the flat with the folded study.** Show front, sides, closure and reading sequence. Use the same panel IDs on the rendered or paper model and the flat, and note which areas are nominal geometry rather than observed folds or seams.

## Limits

A folded drawing or paper dummy explains orientation and visibility, not material behavior, registration or scanner performance. The converter supplies manufacturing evidence and the regulatory owner determines copy obligations by market. GS1 covers barcode applications, not the package’s general labeling rules.

## Worked example

On an illustrative carton, the back panel neighbors the glue flap on the flat. A continuous pattern crosses that edge, but ingredient text and the barcode are kept on the usable back face, outside the converter’s seam exclusion. The top headline appears inverted beside the front in the flat artwork; the paper dummy shows that it reads upright when viewed from the front after closure. The separation schedule names the varnish mask independently so the pattern can continue over the crease without implying that varnish does too.

## Sources

- Norm: [GS1 General Specifications, release 26.0](https://ref.gs1.org/standards/genspecs/26.0.0), application provisions in §2, symbol specifications in §5, placement in §6; `verify` applicable release, tables and subsections for the product and scanner environment.
- Tool: Adobe [Illustrator Help, “About color,” Spot and process colors](https://helpx.adobe.com/nz/illustrator/using/color.html); `verify` installed version for swatch and export behavior.
- Project specification: the issued converter dieline and production brief determine physical allowances, layers and separations.
