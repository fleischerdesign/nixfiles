# Service Design

## When to load & job

Load this for a journey, service blueprint, or prototype that crosses channels, roles, or shifts. A journey shows what the person experiences; the blueprint connects those moments to the backstage work that makes them possible. If the question stays inside one interface, use an interaction module instead.

## Inputs

Use existing touchpoints, service policies, staff accounts, and any journey research or case records the team provides. Identify the people who do the work and the systems they depend on. When records are absent, draft a proposed or hypothetical journey; leave the actual wait, handoff frequency, and current exceptions unknown.

## Decisions & process

Set the journey boundary around the person’s goal when the work crosses channels. Zoom into a single episode when redesigning a handoff, at the cost of losing what the person had already tried. Carry that prior context into the close-up.

Choose who owns the case during a transfer. A warm handoff lets the first team confirm receipt and carry context across, but consumes staff time. An asynchronous queue can absorb uneven demand, but makes ownership easier to lose. In the blueprint, annotate what crosses the line between teams, who acts next, and what the person sees if nobody picks it up.

Build a clickable prototype when wording and status cues are the main unknown. Rehearse with staff when the uncertainty is whether two roles can coordinate. A screen alone cannot show who is available to make a promised callback.

## Typical mistakes & examples

A workshop map ends at “refund submitted,” so the team designs a reassuring confirmation but no route for a stalled payment. The customer calls and retells the story. Extend the blueprint through resolution and add a delayed-payment branch where support can retrieve the case.

## Deliverables & questions for the human

Show the journey, the layered blueprint, and where useful a prototype or rehearsal script. State which backstage steps are simulated. Service staff and researchers can observe the workflow with participants; live case data is needed to establish actual waits and resolution. Ask: Who can accept an exception? What may cross the handoff without making the person repeat it?
