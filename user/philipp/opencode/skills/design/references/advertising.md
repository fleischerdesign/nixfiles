# Advertising

## When to load & job

Use this for a campaign idea and the ads that carry it across placements. The job is to decide what the audience should remember or do after an encounter. Brand design supplies the identity; advertising gives that identity something specific to say now.

## Inputs

Bring the audience and campaign objective, the actual offer and destination, brand assets, and the media team's placement specs. Get claim wording, supporting evidence, and any required qualifiers from the people responsible for the offer. Without those inputs, make a placement study using supplied facts, name the assumed formats, and leave unsupported copy and final exports out.

## Decisions & process

- **Choose the idea before the headline.** If people don't know the product category, lead with a recognizable situation and show how the product changes it. If they're already shopping, lead with the documented difference. The first makes the offer easier to understand but takes longer to get to the point; the second spends less time explaining why anyone should care.
- **Reorder the message by placement.** For a glance-only poster, give the idea a single focal point and keep the brand close to it. In a high-intent placement, bring the concrete offer and next action forward. Keep the campaign recognizable through a shared image or premise, not an identical stack of copy.
- **Scope the claim to the proof.** Put the condition next to the claim when it changes the meaning. If the placement can't hold both, use a narrower supported statement or drop the claim. You give up a punchier headline rather than asking a footnote to change what the headline promises.

## Typical mistakes & examples

Shrinking a paid-social ad to a station poster preserves the button graphic but loses the click. A commuter sees “Learn more” with nowhere to go. Give the poster a legible destination suited to the viewing distance; keep the clickable action for the feed, and name what opens next.

## Deliverables & questions for the human

Show the campaign premise, two placements at their viewing scale, and the action alongside its destination. Note which claims each placement uses and the supplied evidence behind them. Ask: Is this placement meant to introduce the offer or capture existing demand? Which offer will the destination actually honor?
