# Photography & Image Making

## When to load & job

Use this when the decision is which image to use, how to frame it, or what a photographer needs to bring back. Work with real assets and their intended placements. You can edit a selection and mock up crops; a shot brief is not a photoshoot.

Art direction leads when the open question is what treatment unites a series. Here the deliverable is a selection and edit, a placement study, or a brief for capturing a missing frame.

## Inputs & concepts

Get originals or a contact sheet with identifiers, captions and provenance from the photographer or asset owner. Ask the publisher which people, details and relationships the audience must see, and for each destination’s aspect ratio, size, copy area and color requirements. Get usage permissions and edit restrictions from the rights owner. Keep treatment references separate from selectable artwork. If originals or permissions are unavailable, make an annotated placement study rather than releasing reusable files.

**Framing** organizes subject, surroundings and empty space at capture. A **crop** removes parts of that composition; it cannot move two subjects closer or change viewpoint. A **protected detail** is whatever changes the meaning if lost, such as the point where a tool touches its workpiece. **Negative space** can hold copy, but only if the actual letters remain distinct from the texture and tonal shifts behind them. An **edit recipe** records crop, tonal and color adjustments separately from the original so a later placement can start from the full frame.

## Decisions & procedure

- Keep context when the relationship between hand, tool and surface explains the process. Move closer when expression matters more, accepting that the viewer learns less about the setting.
- A wide original can carry an overlay, but a vertical crop may lose another person or change the action. A separate portrait frame costs selection or capture time but preserves the story when a single source cannot.
- An observed moment retains the context of real work while offering less control over light and composition. A directed setup can protect several framings; do not present it as an observed event.

1. Make an **edit map**. For each placement, record the action or expression that must read, the permitted loss of context, and the adjustment the publisher is asking for. Mark editorial restrictions separately from aesthetic preferences.
2. Select and place candidates using [Image selection](knowledge/image-selection.md) (`references/knowledge/image-selection.md`) for comparing frames and recording the crop windows. View the shortlist first as thumbnails to see where attention goes and then with real copy at the intended display size.
3. Keep the source intact. Save placement-specific edit instructions with the asset ID. For tone, use a broad adjustment for the overall relationship between subject and background, and a local adjustment only where a specific detail needs separation; avoid removing context that supplies evidence. For source interpretation and distinct publication outputs, use [Color management](knowledge/color-management.md) (`references/knowledge/color-management.md`) so a review export does not become the master by accident.
4. Where no frame tells the story without an implausible crop or edit, translate the gap into a capture instruction: viewpoint, must-show detail, space for type, alternate orientation and whether direction is permitted.

## Worked example & typical mistakes

A workshop article explains how a guide keeps a cut straight. The horizontal original shows the hand, guide and point of contact, with a quiet wall on the left for the headline. The center portrait crop keeps the bench and cuts away the hand, turning a demonstration into a still life. Moving the portrait window right saves the hand but pushes the contact point against the edge; the reader can no longer tell where the guide meets the work. Keep the wide crop and brief a separate vertical view that places the hand and point of contact one above the other, with the heading outside the photograph if there is no clear space. In the edit map, name the contact point rather than requesting “more workshop detail”; it tells the photographer what determines the angle.

## Standards, limits & handover

Adobe Lightroom Classic Help, “[Crop and Straighten photos](https://helpx.adobe.com/lightroom-classic/help/crop-rotate-straighten.html),” documents aspect-ratio and framing controls; `verify` the installed version. [ICC.1:2022](https://archive.color.org/specification/ICC.1-2022-05.pdf), §6, defines ICC profile and transform concepts, not the preferred look of a photograph. The edit map is a working convention; the delivery profile, size, permitted retouching and rights are project or publisher specifications.

Show the short contact sheet, the selected crops with copy in place, and the edits or assumptions that affect interpretation. A mockup does not establish provenance, permission or what a photographer can capture. Ask which detail can never be cropped out. For a gap, hand over the brief and let the photographer supply the actual frames.
