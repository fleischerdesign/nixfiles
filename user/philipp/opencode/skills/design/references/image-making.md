# Photography & Image Making

## When to load & job

Use this when the decision is which image to use, how to frame it, or what a photographer needs to bring back. Work with real assets and their intended placements. You can edit a selection and mock up crops; a shot brief is not a photoshoot.

## Inputs

Ask for originals or a contact sheet, source and usage permissions, required subjects or details, and each placement’s aspect ratio, size, and copy area. Keep reference images separate from selectable assets. If originals or rights are unavailable, deliver an annotated placement study and request the files or permissions before reuse.

## Decisions & process

Choose between context and intimacy. When the image needs to explain a process, keep the hand, tool, and surface in frame so their relationship reads. If the subject’s expression is the point, move in and accept that the setting may disappear. Compare candidate frames as thumbnails and then at the intended display size.

Crop for meaning before convenience. One wide original with generous negative space can take overlay copy, but its vertical crop may lose the second subject. Make a separate portrait selection if both subjects matter; reuse the original only if the narrower crop still says what the brief needs. Show actual crop boundaries and copy positions on the source.

Decide whether the brief asks for an observed moment or a repeatable setup. An observed moment preserves the context of real work but offers less control over light and composition. A directed setup gives the photographer room to compose for several placements; don’t present a staged scene as documentation.

## Typical mistakes & examples

A center crop on a workshop photo keeps the workbench and cuts off the hand guiding the tool. What looked like a demonstration now reads as a still life. Move the crop toward the action or choose a frame composed around the gesture.

## Deliverables & questions for the human

Show a short contact sheet and selected crops in place. For missing imagery, give the photographer a brief with must-show details, framing alternatives, and the intended uses; the photographer supplies the actual frames for selection. Ask which detail can never be cropped out.
