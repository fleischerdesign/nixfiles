# Design Research

## When to load & job

Use this for a study brief, recruitment plan, session guide, or survey instrument. Work backward from the design decision the team needs to make. A UX prototype gives people something to try; the research brief specifies what trying it could reveal, which people and conditions matter, and what that evidence cannot establish. Prepare a study for a research lead to review and run, not findings in advance or a simulation that stands in for participants.

## Inputs & concepts

Bring the options still open, existing evidence and its provenance, and the deadline for the decision. Ask the research lead for the population of interest, reachable recruitment routes, access to real task materials or records, and consent and data-handling constraints. A **sampling frame** is the source from which people can be selected; it is not necessarily the population you want to understand. A list of account holders excludes people who left before creating an account. If recruitment routes are not established, describe the frame as provisional rather than claiming coverage.

An **intended inference** states what kind of conclusion the evidence might support. Seeing a person use a workaround can reveal a mechanism; hearing their account reveals what they recall or understand; estimating prevalence requires a suitable denominator and defensible coverage. Specify the **unit of observation**, such as a booking attempt rather than a person, when people can contribute more than one episode. State whether existing records track the same unit, start point, and period that the decision concerns.

## Decisions & procedure

- **Situated observation or retrospective account.** Observe actual work when interruptions, tools, or workarounds matter. A recent-episode interview is easier when observation would expose private activity, but reconstruction leaves details uncertain. If the choice depends on prevalence, budget for suitable records or a survey rather than extending a qualitative guide.
- **Available participants or decision-relevant contrasts.** Product recruitment reaches active users quickly. People who switched channels or stopped may require support or community routes and more time; omitting them can hide the decisive mechanism.
- **Comparable structure or room for discovery.** Use the same starting episode and note fields for comparable accounts, with space for context and surprises. A rigid script can impose the researchers’ categories before participants show where the choice happens.

1. Make a **decision-to-evidence map**. For each open choice, record the competing explanations, what observation could distinguish them, and the conclusion the study would not support. Remove questions whose answers would leave the choice unchanged.
2. Use [Research methods](knowledge/research-methods.md) (`references/knowledge/research-methods.md`) to turn the map into method, recruitment matrix, session or survey instrument, and traceable synthesis plan. Carry row labels into the instruments so the research lead can see why each prompt or record request exists.
3. Add an administration note for whoever runs the study. Distinguish an unassisted action from one prompted by the moderator; specify how to handle interruption, requests for help, or materials that contain someone else’s data. Decide which material the study really needs to retain and what can be recorded as a redacted description instead.
4. Attach a blank synthesis worksheet that leaves outcomes open. Give the lead a place to document alternate explanations, evidence gaps, and how findings could change the choice. Keep a sample row visibly fictional if one is needed to explain the format.

## Worked example & typical mistakes

A booking team is deciding between rewriting an eligibility screen and showing appointment availability earlier. Both problems could produce “I stopped on that page.” On the map, distinguish someone who understood eligibility but expected no suitable slots from someone who wrongly assumed they were ineligible. Ask the participant to reconstruct the last attempt in order, pausing before showing the next screen: “What did you expect would happen next?” Keep what they remember separate from anything they discover in the reconstruction. That distinction makes either design change discussable without making a claim about how often the blockage occurs.

Recruiting at confirmation removes unfinished attempts, so the study may polish a stage many affected users never see. Add support and community recruitment, and record where those routes still miss attempts.

## Standards, limits & handover

Beyer and Holtzblatt, *Contextual Design: Defining Customer-Centered Systems* (Morgan Kaufmann, 1998), contextual inquiry chapter (section `verify`), grounds inquiry in work practice. Dillman, Smyth, and Christian, *Internet, Phone, Mail, and Mixed-Mode Surveys: The Tailored Design Method*, 4th ed. (Wiley, 2014), covers survey coverage and mode effects (sections `verify`). These are methods, not norms. Where GDPR applies, Regulation (EU) 2016/679 Art. 5 sets data-processing principles including minimization and storage limitation; `verify` applicable duties and lawful basis with the data owner. Participation consent and data-processing authorization are not interchangeable. Retention and approved tools are project-specific.

Show the human the map, screener, draft participation and consent materials, guide, and blank synthesis sheet. The research lead reviews ethics, runs the study with actual participants, and limits findings to the evidence collected. What choice can the study change? Whose experience would overturn the current assumption?
