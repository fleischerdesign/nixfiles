# Design Research

## When to load & job

Use this for a study brief, recruitment plan, or research instrument. Work backward from the decision the team needs to make. The assignment is to create a way to learn, not to supply findings or stand in for participants.

## Inputs

Bring the decision, existing evidence and how it was collected, what is still unknown, and constraints on recruitment, consent, and data handling. Ask the research lead for the population of interest and the routes available to reach it. Without those, draft questions and a provisional sampling frame, not a claim about coverage.

## Decisions & process

- Match method to the question. Observe a task for workarounds people may not remember to mention; interview when you need the reasoning behind a choice, accepting that recall isn't behavior. If the decision hinges on how often something happens, plan a suitable survey or use production records with a known denominator. A handful of interviews won't estimate frequency.
- Recruit for variation that could change the decision. Regular users are easier to reach through the product; former users or people who never got started require other channels and more time. Use a recruitment matrix to show who each channel leaves out.
- Plan synthesis before fieldwork. A shared observation sheet makes sessions easier to compare; open notes preserve surprises it didn't anticipate. Link interpretations to recorded observations and keep counterexamples visible instead of forcing them into the dominant theme.

## Typical mistakes & examples

Recruiting only from a booking confirmation screen removes people who couldn't finish, so the study may focus on polishing confirmation instead of finding barriers earlier. Recruit through support and community channels as well, and ask people to walk through their last booking attempt.

## Deliverables & questions for the human

Show the decision-to-question map, screener, draft consent materials, session guide, and blank synthesis worksheet. The research lead reviews ethics and runs the study with real participants; findings and their limits come from collected evidence. What decision could this study change? Whose experience would overturn the team's current assumption?
