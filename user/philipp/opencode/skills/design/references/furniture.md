# Furniture

## When to load & job

Use this for a chair, table, storage piece, or furniture family. Start with the relationship between the body, the piece, and whatever it sits beside. Use interior when the room is the main deliverable and the furniture is one of several elements in it.

## Inputs

Gather intended postures and durations, user needs, neighboring furniture dimensions, available floor area, and delivery and storage routes. Ask for measured dimensions, including underside obstructions, not just catalog footprints. Without them, draw body and furniture envelopes with assumptions labeled; leave the final dimensions open.

## Decisions & process

- For a shared workstation, consider adjustment where people differ most in posture or reach; the price is more controls and parts to maintain. Where users can choose between pieces, several fixed sizes may be simpler, but they take more floor and storage space. Show who can use which setup in side elevation.
- For a communal table, an uninterrupted edge makes it easier to add a place setting, but the support arrangement still affects knees and feet. A perimeter leg can free the middle while making corner seats awkward; a central base reverses some of that trade. Draw occupied seats and feet, not just the tabletop.
- Decide whether the piece lives in one place or is frequently stored. A stackable chair frees floor area between sessions, but stacking geometry can constrain seat shape and back support. Compare occupied and stored silhouettes, including how staff move the chairs.

## Typical mistakes & examples

Matching a seat to the advertised desk height ignores the deep apron under the desktop. A person can sit at the right height and still be unable to pull close. Draw the seat, thigh, apron, and work surface in one section before choosing a pairing.

## Deliverables & questions for the human

Show a concept brief, annotated plan and section, and a comparison of use and storage positions. Ask which posture dominates and who moves the piece. A furniture specialist leads a full-scale fit study with representative users; the producer supplies evidence on stability, loads, joints, and production constraints. Final dimensions, construction details, and fabrication remain outside the study.
