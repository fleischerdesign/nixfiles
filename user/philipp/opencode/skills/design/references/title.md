# Title Design

## When to load & job

Use this when words are the event: an opening title, a credit sequence, or animated chapter cards. Decide how type enters the story, becomes readable, and hands attention back to the picture. If the question is which shots tell the story, film and video leads.

## Inputs

Bring approved wording and billing order where available, the type system, picture before and after the sequence, and any cue track. Take frame boundaries, aspect ratios, caption placement, and credit constraints from the delivery brief or producer. If copy or picture is provisional, make a timing study with marked stand-ins; exact holds and picture handoffs remain open.

## Decisions & process

- **Hold or weave.** Set a clear hold for long or unfamiliar names. Count reading time from when the whole line is legible, not from when the first letter arrives. A shorter, familiar title can emerge through movement, but that leaves less time for the viewer to look at the picture.
- **Separate or embed.** Give titles their own card when footage is dense or crops vary; the trade-off is a pause in the action. Embed them when the scene gives type a meaningful plane or edge. Lay out the longest line over the actual shot and across required crops before committing to the transition.
- **Land on the cue or let it lead.** Time a main reveal to a sound accent when the title should feel like a single arrival. Let the cue precede the type if you want anticipation. Keep placement and timing expressive without sound when muted playback matters.

## Typical mistakes & examples

A credit reveals letter by letter and dissolves as soon as the last letter lands. The name is never fully present long enough to read. Shorten the build, hold the complete name, and put the more elaborate move on the exit.

## Deliverables & questions for the human

Show the sequence with the preceding and following shots, plus a timing strip marking entry, readable hold, and exit. Ask whether the title should interrupt the story or feel like part of the scene. Request outstanding credit copy and billing order from the producer.
