# Creative Direction

## When to load & job

Use this for a campaign concept or a direction that writers, designers, and channel owners need to interpret together. Decide what the audience should take away and what idea carries it. Art direction gives that idea a visual treatment; this brief should survive a change in treatment.

## Inputs

Bring the audience and occasion, the action the brief asks for, the offer or story, known objections, and claims the owner can substantiate. Use the project’s voice and brand constraints. If audience evidence is missing, name the assumption and frame the directions as hypotheses. Keep unsupported claims out of the examples.

## Decisions & process

Pick the tension the campaign will resolve. If people understand the offer but doubt it, lead with a demonstrable truth; you may give up surprise to make the claim concrete. If the brief is about making an unfamiliar offer imaginable, lead with a recognizable situation; you give up some space for explaining how it works. Compare both as a proposition, a sample opening, and a next action.

Decide whether the idea travels as a repeated line or as a repeatable device. A line carries the same message through brief encounters but wears thin if every placement does the same job. A device can unfold in different ways: a tool library might ask “What would you fix?” on a poster, then answer with available tools on the landing page. That gives each placement a role but makes the concept depend more on coordination.

## Typical mistakes & examples

When every channel gets the launch headline and nothing else, a person who follows the poster link meets the same promise instead of learning how to borrow. Give the poster the invitation and the landing page the actual borrowing steps supplied by the owner.

## Deliverables & questions for the human

Show two concept routes in the same placements, the recommended trade-off, and a short brief naming what stays constant. Ask which audience hesitation the campaign should address first. The owner supplies claim evidence; a research or campaign lead can gather audience response. Leave effectiveness and unsupported claims unresolved.
