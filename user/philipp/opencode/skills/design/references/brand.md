# Brand & Corporate

## When to load & job

Lead with Brand for an identity system or its guidelines: turn agreed positioning into names, signatures, type, color and voice that different teams can use across touchpoints. Corporate is an alias for Brand. Deliver a system study with representative applications, or reusable masters and rules when the identity direction is established. If the task is a single campaign expression rather than the reusable system beneath it, lead with Advertising.

## Inputs & concepts

Get the organization's positioning, approved claims, audience evidence, existing assets and names, and the actual relationship among offers, parents and partners. Collect templates from the teams who make notices, receipts, partner materials and product screens. Ask which decisions those teams can make locally. Where positioning or evidence is missing, label the identity directions as hypotheses, not as discoveries about what customers recognize or prefer.

**Brand architecture** determines whose name leads and when another name endorses it; it need not mirror the organization chart. A **signature** is an assembly, such as a mark with a name or descriptor, with defined relationships among parts. A **fixed cue** carries recognition when channels change, while a **variable cue** makes room for offers, messages or contexts. Voice has a similar split: stance can remain direct and helpful while a service notice calls for a quieter register than a launch.

## Decisions & procedure

- **Parent or offer first.** Lead with the parent when its existing recognition should transfer across offers. Give the offer more prominence when people need to identify it independently; that weakens the immediate family cue. Put sibling offers side by side in the same setting.
- **Wordmark or compact signature.** Favor the written name when it already does the identifying. Explore a symbol if channels routinely strip the name or squeeze it into an avatar, but the symbol asks audiences to learn an association.
- **Fixed signal or flexible expression.** Reserve a palette role or a typographic relationship for continuity, and give secondary accents or image treatments room to vary. If hue already communicates status in a product, do not ask it to identify the offer too.

1. Use [Identity systems](knowledge/identity-systems.md) (`references/knowledge/identity-systems.md`) to turn offer relationships into a **touchpoint matrix** for two structurally different directions. Populate the same directory, avatar, monochrome invoice and signature with actual names; document where a relationship needs to be explicit and where it can recede.
2. Develop the chosen direction as an assembly, not just a logo file. Draw normal, compact and one-color signatures as needed; show which portion leads, how the descriptor attaches and where a partner name goes. Use [Type setting](knowledge/type-setting.md) (`references/knowledge/type-setting.md`) to set the reusable name, heading and text roles against real copy, including long names and required scripts.
3. Assign colors to roles and separate review appearance from production values. Use [Color management](knowledge/color-management.md) (`references/knowledge/color-management.md`) to document destination encodings and brief any stock-dependent color sample. Write usage rules around actual decisions and include editable masters, named exports and contrasting voice examples.

## Worked example & typical mistakes

A repair cooperative sells workshop courses and also organizes neighborhood repair visits. In a combined directory the two services need distinct lead names, but on a reimbursement receipt the cooperative needs prominence as the issuer. Give the matrix separate “audience task” and “issuer” columns. Use “Repair Visits” or “Workshop Courses” as the directory's lead with the same parent endorsement; invert that relationship on the receipt and put the service in a named field. Document two signature arrangements rather than multiplying logos for every service.

Making hue the sole difference may look clear on slides but collapse on a grayscale invoice. Carry the distinction in the name and its position before giving each offer an accent.

## Standards, limits & handover

Wally Olins, *Corporate Identity: Making Business Strategy Visible Through Design* (Thames and Hudson, 1989; `verify` section on identity structure), provides an organizational model, not a norm. NASA's [*Graphics Standards Manual*, NHB 1430.2](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2015/01/nasa_graphics_manual_nhb_1430-2_jan_1976.pdf) (1976), §1.2, is a historical example of codifying parent and unit identification. Signature spacing and partner arrangements are project conventions, not borrowed dimensions. [ICC.1:2022](https://archive.color.org/specification/ICC.1-2022-05.pdf), §6, describes profile architecture, not a brand's approved appearance.

Show the same touchpoints across directions, then hand over guideline examples, asset ownership and unresolved exceptions. Ask whether a new offer should borrow the parent's recognition or build its own. Route trademark and naming clearance to the owner's legal team, and obtain production color evidence from the supplier.
