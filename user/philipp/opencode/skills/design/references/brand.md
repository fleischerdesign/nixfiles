# Brand & Corporate

## When to load & job

Use this for an identity system or its guidelines: positioning translated into a mark, palette, and voice that hold together across touchpoints. Corporate is an alias for Brand. If the job is a single campaign expression rather than a system others will reuse, lead with Advertising.

## Inputs

Use the organization’s agreed positioning, audience and offer, approved claims, current assets, brand relationships, and the places the identity must appear. Without agreed positioning, label directions as hypotheses and use them to clarify the brief; don’t present audience preferences or market distinctions as findings.

## Decisions & process

- **Parent or offer first.** Lead with the parent mark when an existing relationship should carry recognition from one offer to the next. Give a sub-brand more room when people need to identify the offer independently, at the cost of a weaker family signal. Put sibling offers side by side on the same directory or storefront.
- **Where recognition lives.** If the name does the work, favor a wordmark and let typography and color carry more of the system. If space routinely drops the name, explore a compact symbol alongside it; that gives the avatar a usable shape but asks people to learn the association. Compare an avatar, a monochrome invoice, and a large signature rather than judging marks on a blank sheet.
- **What varies.** Keep a core palette role fixed and vary secondary accents when parts of the organization need differentiation. If accents already mean something in a product, let the name or layout carry the distinction instead. Give voice the same structure: keep the stance consistent, but adjust the register between a launch headline and a service notice.

## Typical mistakes & examples

Giving each division its own color as the sole identifier works on presentation slides, then fails on a grayscale invoice where the divisions look identical. Use distinct names in a common lockup and make color a secondary cue.

## Deliverables & questions for the human

Show identity directions across shared touchpoints and a short guideline draft with mark, palette, and voice examples. Ask whether a new offer should borrow the parent’s recognition or build its own. Route mark clearance to the owner’s legal team before adoption.
