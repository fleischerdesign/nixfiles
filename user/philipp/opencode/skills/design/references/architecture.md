# Architecture

## When to load & job

Use this to brief a building or addition: what spaces it contains, how they relate across levels, and how someone moves from the street to the rooms they need. Leave room finishes and loose furniture to an interior study unless they explain why the program needs a particular space.

## Inputs

Gather activities and operating hours, known area needs, existing drawings, and site conditions. Ask the owner for the intended occupancy and a surveyor for boundaries and levels. Ask the project architect to identify the applicable site and regulatory constraints. If these inputs are missing, use an adjacency diagram and a schematic section; label assumed levels and avoid fixing the footprint.

## Decisions & process

- Stack related rooms when land is constrained; spread them on one level when daily movement between them matters more. Stacking can reserve more ground for outdoor use but makes those relationships depend on vertical circulation. Draw the busiest trips in section alongside the area program.
- Give after-hours activities an independent threshold if the main building will be closed. A shared lobby saves duplicate arrival space but makes the building harder to open in parts. Show where visitors, staff, and deliveries enter in both operating modes.
- Put rooms needing quiet or privacy behind a transition rather than directly on the main public route. An offset or intermediate room gives more separation but uses space and may make the destination less obvious. Sketch the view from the public threshold before assigning the room.

## Typical mistakes & examples

A plan places the hall next to the courtyard and treats the adjacency as a direct connection. On a sloping site the courtyard may sit well below the hall, making the intended everyday spill-out awkward. Draw a section from street to hall to courtyard before committing to that relationship.

## Deliverables & questions for the human

Show the room program, adjacency options, and sections through the important thresholds. Ask which rooms must operate independently and which links need to be direct. The project architect, surveyor, and engineers provide the measured site basis and investigate building and regulatory feasibility. Technical design, construction documentation, and any permit submission remain outside the study.
