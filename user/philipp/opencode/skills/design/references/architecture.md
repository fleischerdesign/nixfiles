# Architecture

## When to load & job

Use this to brief a building or addition: what spaces it contains, which relationships must survive changes of level or operating hours, and how people move from the street to the rooms they need. Lead with interior instead if the shell, levels, and building-wide access remain fixed and the issue is room arrangement or finishes. Deliver a program and schematic plan and section options for the project architect, not a design for construction or permit submission.

## Inputs & concepts

Get activities, opening hours, intended occupancy, staffing, deliveries, and known area needs from the owner and operators. Request the surveyor's site boundary, street and ground levels with their datum, and the source and date of existing drawings. Ask the project architect which planning, building, and access rules govern the study. If this basis is absent, leave footprint and assumed levels open and work with relationships, not precise-looking floor plans.

**Programmed area** is area allocated to the listed functions; it is not the whole building area. Say where shared circulation, structure, services, and other allowances are carried instead of treating the room total as a footprint. A **threshold** can be a door, level change, line of supervision, or switch between public and restricted use. A connection can be near in plan but indirect in section, or available by day but closed at night. Distinguish a program request for ordinary access from an egress obligation; a diagram of access control cannot settle the latter.

## Decisions & procedure

- **Stacking versus single-level links.** Stack to preserve limited ground or outdoor area; spread when a frequent movement or shared equipment needs a level relationship. Stacking transfers some of the daily trip to vertical circulation.
- **Shared arrival versus independent opening.** A common lobby uses less duplicate arrival space, while an independent threshold can let one use operate without opening the rest. Separate access may add staffing or leave frontage unavailable to another use.
- **Public visibility versus a buffered transition.** A visible doorway helps people find a destination, but a quiet or private use may need a turn or an intermediate space. The buffer takes area and changes what visitors see on arrival.

1. Use [Spatial programming](knowledge/spatial-programming.md) (`references/knowledge/spatial-programming.md`) to develop the room program and relationships from operations. Carry each consequential relationship into a named trip, such as arrival, loaded movement, or shared use of an outdoor area.
2. Make a **threshold schedule** for those trips. Give each crossing an origin, destination, operating mode, level assumption, boundary type, and dependence on a shared facility or staff action. Distinguish visual connection from actual movement.
3. Draw two massing or stacking options on the same site base. Mark which thresholds move and follow the same trips in each. Cut a section from the relevant approach through the building to its outside destination; use the survey datum where supplied and show level differences without implying a resolved vertical route.
4. Present the options with their space programs and day and after-hours diagrams. Note any dependence on structure, services, earthwork, or external ownership beside the line it affects so downstream consultants know what assumption their evidence would replace.

## Worked example & typical mistakes

A community hall is shown beside a courtyard intended for everyday spill-out. The schedule splits “hall to courtyard” into see the activity outside, walk out during an event, and take furniture out during setup. On the slope, a section reveals the courtyard is below the proposed hall floor. A view through glazing could preserve the first relationship without solving either movement. Keeping the hall near the street arrival level preserves a short street-to-hall link but leaves a courtyard transition to resolve. Moving the hall to the courtyard level puts that transition on the street approach instead and changes which spaces share the same floor. Draw both sections with the same surveyed or provisional ground line; the owner can then say whether setup and public spill-out need the same connection.

“Adjacent” on a plan is often mistaken for “direct.” The effect may be an everyday trip routed through the building's circulation. Name the task at the threshold, and draw the ground and occupied levels before locking in the relationship.

## Standards, limits & handover

Peña and Parshall, *Problem Seeking*, 5th edition (2012), separates architectural programming from design; `verify` the relevant chapter. As an example of a model code, the ICC *2024 International Building Code*, chapters 10 (means of egress) and 11 (accessibility), shows why operating diagrams cannot stand in for those analyses. `Verify` the locally adopted edition, scope, and amendments with the architect. An owner area cap is a project specification, threshold labels are a drawing convention, and the hall section is illustrative.

Show the program, options, and sections through decisive thresholds. Ask which rooms must operate independently and which links need to be direct for which activity. The architect, surveyor, and engineers provide the measured site basis and investigate regulatory and technical feasibility. Technical design, construction documentation, and permit submission remain outside the study.
