# Transportation

## When to load & job

Use this for a vehicle or mobility concept whose layout must serve a trip: boarding, riding or driving, loading, and servicing. Turn the intended use into packaging and interface questions. The work concerns priorities and their consequences, not vehicle engineering.

## Inputs

Ask for the duty cycle, users and operators, typical loads, boarding locations, service routines, and any known platform constraints. Take hard points and dimensional envelopes from the vehicle team. If they are unavailable, sketch relative zones and movement paths, labeling assumed geometry rather than assigning clearances.

## Decisions & process

- For frequent curbside stops, give boarding, circulation, and stowage priority near the door. For longer trips, allocating more of that area to seating may be worth it, at the cost of room for people and gear to move together. Draw the busiest boarding moment and the settled ride on the same package sketch.
- If the vehicle alternates between passengers and cargo, compare a flexible shared bay with dedicated zones. Shared space makes room for changing loads but adds a changeover task; dedicated space reduces negotiation at each stop but consumes volume even when empty.
- Separate what the driver needs while moving from what the operator can do while parked. Keeping frequent driving actions distinct from setup can reduce screen navigation, but adds controls or takes space from other information. Storyboard a trip and hand attention, reach, and feedback questions to the human-factors specialist.

## Typical mistakes & examples

A shuttle layout shows a clear aisle only with everyone seated → a passenger loading luggage occupies the space needed by someone boarding → draw bags, standing passengers, and door movement in the same frame and consider giving stowage a separate approach.

## Deliverables & questions for the human

Show scenario boards, comparative package sketches, and open safety and interface questions. Vehicle engineers provide packaging evidence; the human-factors team observes tasks in a representative mockup. Dimensions, safety conclusions, and engineering decisions remain open. Which combination of passengers and loads must the concept prioritize, and where does that trip begin?
